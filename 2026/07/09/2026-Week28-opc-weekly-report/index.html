1.2 OPC Numbers Enter a Hyper-Growth Channel

Public data shows that in 2025, newly registered One-Person Companies in China surpassed 7.3 million. Entering 2026, driven by maturing AI tooling and falling startup barriers, OPC entered a high-growth trajectory. The Greater Bay Area has become a bellwether: at the Foshan startup incubation base, the founder of Yaojing Culture uses a self-built AI platform to complete the full pipeline of anime short dramas (script → image → video); a former senior engineer in Shenzhen, Liu Yefei, now runs a business with 5 “AI employees” handling solution planning, coding, code review, deployment, and investment research.

1.3 OPC Communities Evolve from “Solo Ventures” to “Ecosystem Collaboration”

In 2026, OPC communities are evolving from mere co-working spaces into new organizational vehicles that connect AI tools, individual entrepreneurs, industry resources, and enterprise application scenarios. The core shift: a one-person company no longer means “one person fighting alone,” but rather leveraging AI tools and community networks to validate projects with far lighter weight.

2. Tech Stack: Running a Five-Person Team on One Laptop

2.1 The Real Solo-Founder Stack Emerges

Multiple case studies reveal the standardized 2026 toolchain for solo entrepreneurship: a SaaS product that once required a 5-person team (frontend, backend, UI, QA, PM) can now be taken from ideation to launch by one person within 48 hours. The core logic is “Intent-Driven” development, not simple code completion.

  • Product/UI → v0.dev / Lovable (interactive prototypes & React-style code)
  • Full-stack → Cursor / TRAE (Chinese Vibe Coding iteration)
  • One-click gen → bolt.new / replit (website / App / game delivery)

4. Methodology: How Ordinary People Capture the Dividend

4.1 “Manageable Radius” Is the Essence of OPC’s Viability

The OPC worth discussing in 2026 is not “one person doing every function,” but one person learning to design task flows and then running them with AI Agents, automation tools, and external service networks. For the first time, AI has enlarged an ordinary person’s “manageable radius” — the root reason OPC suddenly became real.

4.2 The “Boring but Painful” Niche-Problem Method

The starting point for success is finding “boring but painful” niche problems — usually in traditional industries, SMB operations, and repetitive knowledge work:

  • Legal assistant: auto-generate first drafts of court filings
  • Real-estate agent: auto-write regulation-compliant listings
  • Medical institutions: automate patient follow-up scripts and appointment reminders
  • Marketer: batch-generate on-brand social copy

Channels to spot opportunities: scan complaints and help requests on industry forums, Reddit, Quora, and Twitter/X using Perplexity, Consensus, and Jasper Research.

4.3 The “Virtual Co-Founder” Concept

As a virtual co-founder, AI has three advantages: no emotional volatility, never quits, and avoids cofounder drama. It forms an efficient “AI → founder → user” innovation loop with the solo founder: AI executes labor-intensive tasks, the founder focuses on strategy and product direction, and rapid iteration is driven by user feedback.

5. Risk Notes

⚠️ Important Caveats
① OPC is not a “zero-cost myth”: compute, API calls, compliance, and tax remain real expenses — “one-person company” ≠ “zero-risk company.”
② Tooling iterates extremely fast; betting on a single platform creates lock-in / replacement risk — keep your toolchain swappable.
③ Copyright and data-compliance requirements for AI-generated content are tightening worldwide — confirm local regulations before launch.
④ This report is trend monitoring only and is not investment advice. Entrepreneurship carries risk; proceed with caution, don’t go all-in blindly.
