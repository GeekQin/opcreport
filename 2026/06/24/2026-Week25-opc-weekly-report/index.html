OPC Goes Institutional: Policy Push, 6 Proven Monetization Paths — W25 Report

Week 25 (June 18 – 24, 2026) — This week marked a turning point: Guangdong became China’s first province to release a dedicated AI OPC action plan, WAIC announced a global OPC startup competition, and at least six clear monetization paths have now been proven in the wild. The OPC movement is no longer a fringe concept — it’s receiving institutional backing at unprecedented speed.

🔥 Top Stories This Week

🏛️ Guangdong Releases Landmark AI OPC Action Plan

Guangdong Province published the “Supporting AI OPC Innovation Development Action Plan (2026-2028)” — the first provincial-level OPC-specific policy document in China. The targets are ambitious: 100 OPC ecological communities by 2028, nurturing 1,000 benchmark enterprises and aggregating 10,000 AI entrepreneurship talents. Computing subsidies, physical space support, financing channels, and talent programs are all on the table — a full-stack policy package.

What this means: Provincial backing signals that OPC has moved from grassroots experimentation to formal economic strategy. If you’ve been waiting for a sign to start, this is it.

🌐 WAIC 2026 Launches First-Ever OPC Track

The World Artificial Intelligence Conference (WAIC) partnered with OpenCSG to launch the “OPC Independent Pioneer Challenge” — the world’s first top-tier competition dedicated to super-individual AI entrepreneurs. The finals will be held at the WAIC 2026 venue in Shanghai’s Expo Center this July. Data cited by the organizers shows that over 1/3 of new companies are now solo-founded, with a 53% increase in the solo-founder share over 6 years.

The core logic driving this: “Carbon-based wisdom (human) + Silicon-based execution (AI) = A complete company.”

🛠️ Domestic LLMs Make AI Accessible to Everyone

China’s domestic large language models are advancing rapidly, with DeepSeek and others driving down inference costs while approaching global frontier performance. The “tech democratization” effect is real: content creation efficiency has transformed, customer service capabilities have jumped exponentially, and data analysis support is now accessible to solo founders who previously couldn’t afford such tools.

💵 6 Proven Monetization Paths (Backed by Real Cases)

Path 1: The “Shovel Seller” — AI Tool Deployment

Help non-technical individuals and SMBs set up AI environments, deploy agents, and configure workflows.

Revenue model: ¥100–500 per deployment session
Monthly ceiling: ¥10,000+ (part-time)
Real case: A 27-year-old in Wuhan taught clients how to “raise a digital lobster” (set up AI digital employees) and cashed in ¥100,000 in one week. Weekend programmers picking up OpenClaw deployment gigs routinely hit five-figure monthly income.

Path 2: The “Super Individual” — AI Replaces an Entire Team

One person + multi-agent workflow = what used to require a team of dozens.

Revenue model: Content monetization, brand deals, annual retainers
Real case: Huang Tao in Zhengzhou spent one hour training an AI assistant named “Tomato” and now runs 7 WeChat public accounts single-handedly. Topic selection, writing, formatting, contracts — all AI-managed. One article landed a ¥500,000 annual partnership.

Path 3: Custom Agent Development for SMBs

Build customer service agents, sales assistants, content tools, and data processing pipelines for small businesses.

Revenue model: ¥5,000–50,000 per project + SaaS subscriptions
Scalability: One person can manage 50+ AI digital employees simultaneously

Path 4: AI Content Entrepreneurship + Multi-Platform Distribution

Automate topic selection, writing, editing, and publishing across platforms.

Efficiency gain: 5x content output increase
Market signal: AI-related job postings are up 12x YoY, with average monthly salaries exceeding ¥60,000

Path 5: AI Music / Copyright Monetization

A niche but fast-growing segment. A Shenzhen-based post-90s creator using AI to compose music has already earned five-figure royalties.

Path 6: Vertical SaaS One-Person Unicorn

Deep industry expertise + AI coding tools = no need for large engineering teams. Founder focuses on strategy while AI handles execution.

⚡ Tech Trends Reshaping OPC

Multi-Agent Architecture Goes Mainstream

Gartner predicts 70% of enterprise AI applications will adopt multi-agent architectures by 2026. The shift is from “tuning models” to “designing agent collaboration flows” — agents autonomously divide tasks and coordinate decisions.

Desktop AI Agents Arrive

Laiye Technology launched Laiye Worker, a desktop agent product, along with an “AI Employee Family Bucket” covering finance, HR, marketing, and customer service. AI agents are moving from tech validation to actual enterprise job positions.

Native Multimodal: AI Understands the World

Models like GPT-4o, Sora 2, and Meta Muse Spark process text, images, audio, video, and 3D simultaneously from the architecture level up. One-person companies can now create rich multimedia content that previously required a full creative team.

🧠 Lao Qin’s Take

“The OPC playbook for 2026 is clear — it’s not about who has the deepest tech. It’s about who can close a commercial loop first using AI.”

Two entry paths stand out:

  • For quick cash: Be a “shovel seller” — deploy AI tools for people who can’t do it themselves. Lowest barrier, fastest validation.
  • For the long game: Become a “super individual” — train AI to be your digital twin and scale your output by 10x.

Whichever path you choose, one rule applies: Don’t YOLO into it. Validate the smallest unit of your business first, then scale.

⚠️ Risk check: Policy rollout timelines vary by region. AI tools evolve fast — expect ongoing learning investment. One-person companies have limited risk-bearing capacity — test as a side hustle before going full-time. And never ignore compliance (data privacy, IP rights).
